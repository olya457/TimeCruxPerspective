1. The City Offer, With a Parent Who Is Getting Older
Tags: Family, Career Depth: 4/4
Situation:  You receive a strong job offer in another city. It would increase your income and responsibility, but one of your parents is getting older and currently lives close enough that you can help when needed. Nobody is asking you to stay, but you know distance will change what “being available” means.
Intro:  Every option costs something real. Choose the compromise you are most willing to live with — there is no correct answer.
Option 1 — Take the offer
Short description:  Higher pay, bigger role, less presence.
Gains:
 +38% income
 Stronger career trajectory
 New professional network
Costs:
 Less time with family
 Unfamiliar city
 Less ability to help spontaneously
Risk: Medium–High
Option 2 — Stay and renegotiate
Short description:  Known ground, slower climb.
Gains:
 Stay close to family
 Keep your support network
 More predictable year
Costs:
 Slower career growth
 The salary gap remains
 You may wonder what you missed
Risk: Low–Medium
Option 3 — Ask for six months
Short description:  Buy time, risk the opportunity.
Gains:
 More information
 Time to prepare your family
 Chance to test alternatives
Costs:
 Offer may disappear
 Months of uncertainty
 Decision remains unresolved
Risk: Medium
Set Your Priorities
Rate:
 Being near family
 Income growth
 Career challenge
 Stability
 Independence
Most Important to Me:  Presence / Ambition / Security / Autonomy
Confidence: 0–100%
Result Example
You chose presence over pace.
You prioritised:  Being reachable when your family needs you.
You accepted:  A slower professional climb, at least for now.
Values behind the choice:  Presence — High  Security — Medium–High  Ambition — Medium
Another way to read it:  Staying can be an act of care, but it can also be the option that asks the fewest uncomfortable questions. Leaving would not necessarily mean choosing career over family; it could also mean trusting that closeness is not measured only in kilometres.
